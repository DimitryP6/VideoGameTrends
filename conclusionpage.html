Conclusion

We noticed a few key findings:
  1. Despite popular online drama about video-game critics poorly representing popular games, the dataset showed otherwise. There was a positive correlation between user rating and critic rating for video games, and this rating had an especially strong correlation for those games that had large sales.
  2. While Japan is a big producer of Video-Games, their market produces far less income than across Europe, North America, and other countries. The only game genres in which Japanese consumer sales exceed those of the others is for fighting games, platformers, and especially role-playing games.
  3. Many video-game companies produce only specific games, and many platforms promote specific companies. For example, XBox promotes EA games, which are mainly sports games Therefore, gaming culture looks different in Northern America where XBox may have a larger share of the market in contrast to Japan, where Nintendo and associated role-playing games have a larger share.
If our analysis were to be improved, here is what we think are the main points:
  1. It would have been good to also consider marketing and general feedback from consumers, beacuse that would also have a lot to say about video game culture in certain regions and how video games are sold there. For this level of analysis, the dataset would have to have been expanded by scraping perhaps online gaming forums like Reddit and Steam.
  2. More in-depth research on the topic would help destinguish between causation and correlation relationships. For example, although there is a positive correlation between user rating and critic score, it is unclear whether perhaps critic score follows user rating, if user rating is influenced by critic score, or if there is another factor.

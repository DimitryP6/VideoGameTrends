Analyzing the Relationship Between a Game's Critic Score, User score, and Revenue

Critic Score vs User Score Visualization

This interactive dashboard combines a scatterplot and bar chart to show how critic scores, user scores, and revenue relate across games. Each point represents a game, with critic score on the x-axis and user score on the y-axis to reveal correlations, clusters, and outliers. Color represents ESRB rating, and point size reflects global sales, highlighting titles with greater commercial impact.

To support exploration, the visualization provides multiple interactive filters, including genre, rating, developer, platform, and minimum global sales. This allows users to search for specific subsets of the data or to compare groups. This makes the tool useful for high-level analysis tasks such as discovering which types of games tend to receive high ratings from both critics and players, identifying market trends, or examining how content rating relates to commercial performance.

Key Takeaways

  1. Games rated E (Everyone) have the highest global sales at $1,979.71M, likely due to broad accessibility. They are followed by M (Mature) at $1,437.10M, T (Teen) at $1,396.11M, and E10+ at $553.08M
  2. Using the revenue slider to display the top 10 highest-grossing video games reveals that the Wii Sport franchise holds 4 titles (Wii Sports, Wii Sports Resort, Wii Fit, Wii Fit Plus) partly because many were bundled with the console, which boosted accessibility significantly.
  3. There is a moderate positive correlation between critic scores and user scores, indicating that games well-received by critics also tend to be favored by players.
  4. Across all regions, North America generates the highest revenue, followed by Europe, while Japan consistently ranks the lowest total sales.
  5. Overall, the dashboard shows that global revenue is heavily concentrated in games that score well with both critics and users, meaning commercial success tends to align with strong reception rather than genre or rating alone

Critic Score vs User Score Visualization for Two Genre Comparison

This visualization compares two selected genres by showing how user scores, critic scores, and review counts differ between them. The boxplots highlight differences in spread, medians, and outliers for both user and critic reviews. The accompanying the summary table aggregates key metrics such as average scores, total review counts, and average global sales. This summary helps address a shortfall in the first visualization by placing review counts and score scales in direct relation to one another. This allows for a more balanced comparison of genres that may differ widely in popularity and sample size.

To support exploration, the dropdown menu enables users to switch between 2 different genres and directly compare how their review distributions change across categories. This makes the visualization useful for identifying which genres have more consistent ratings, which attract greater user or critic engagement, and whether certain genres show a stronger correlation between critic and player sentiment.

Key Takeaways

  1. Across all genres the average user score is 72.46, the average user count is 100,539, the average critic score is 70.73, the average critic count is 16,698, and average global sales rest at $0.721 million,
  2. Role-playing games have the highest average user rating (76.135), while shooter games have the lowest on average (70.76).
  3. Shooter games have the highest critic score on average (74.165), while adventure games have the lowest critics score on average (66.177)
  4. Platform and shooter games have very similar average global sales ($0.931 million and $0.934 million respectfully) which are the highest in the dataset. The genre with the lowest average global sales are strategy games with $0.262 million
  5. Platform games appear to be the most attractive genre. With an average user rating of 73.8, an average critic score of 70.03, an average global sales of $0.931 million, and high engagement with both critics in users, platform games perform well across all metrics.
